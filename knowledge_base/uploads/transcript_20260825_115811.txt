## Openness to the Working of the Universe

There is still this notion—embracing the workings of the universe, which doesn’t necessarily mean sitting quietly with closed eyes as Taoists do. However, there's the idea that observing the stillness of mind can be combined with action in such a way that when we are completely present, our actions will proceed effortlessly and smoothly, without friction, and accompanied by a sharp focus so intense it feels almost ecstatic.

### Bill Russell’s Experience

Retired professional basketball player Bill Russell writes about times he experienced this state where things felt almost as if played in slow motion. During these moments, the game seemed to unfold more predictably; he could almost foresee what would happen next and where the next shot would be taken.

## The Flow State: Ting’s Cookery

The story of **Ting**, a professional cook who works in a state of flow, is remarkable. He was observed by an amazed Lord who remarked on his elegant skill with the knife. When questioned about why he cut so beautifully, the cook replied, “I care not about the skill itself; what matters to me is the way it should be done.” Initially, Ting saw only the box he was cutting up. After three years of practice, however, he no longer saw the old box but instead focused on how things naturally unfold. Now, his technique had moved beyond mere skill into a state where perception and understanding merged with spirit, allowing him to move along with the natural flow without being bound by sight or physical action.

### The Significance of the Tool

The story emphasizes that a crucial aspect is the condition of the knife Ting uses; good cooks change their knives every year due to intense use. On the other hand, mediocre cooks replace theirs monthly because they hack at things forcefully. **Ting**, however, had used the same knife for nineteen years without changing it because he wielded it with great skill and subtlety.

## The Teaching of Taoism: Power of Gentleness

### Non-Action as a Path

An essential part of this teaching is the concept of gentleness within Taoism. While forcing and striving might get things done, they often expend more energy than necessary and can lead to collateral damage. On the other hand, operating in a state akin to flow allows one to find balance between action and non-action. This approach aims for a balanced state—neither too hot nor too cold—which finds a path between anxiety and danger.

### The Contrast with Western Ideal

The idea of “non-action” fundamentally differs from a Western ideal that encourages relentless pushing and working harder and harder to achieve results. Taoism’s perspective suggests there is wisdom in knowing when to act rather than always striving for progress, understanding that sometimes less force or pressure can result in greater effectiveness and harmony.
